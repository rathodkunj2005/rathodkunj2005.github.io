Talent / Research

Mentored research and long-running technical projects

BioGraphRAG: biomedical question answering (GMG Summer of Code, May–Aug 2024)

Objective. Let a model answer biomedical questions with grounded facts instead of plausible guesses. Method. I led a GraphRAG platform over 1M+ biomedical entities from UniProt, AlphaFold, RXNav, and BioKG, stored in NebulaGraph and distributed for traversal speed. Outcome. 40% higher factual accuracy than the baseline retriever, about 3× faster graph traversal, and sub-500ms p95 queries. I wrote it up as an article that NebulaGraph's team later republished.

Theme tie. Health informatics: drug and protein knowledge is only usable at the bedside if retrieval is correct and traceable.

Predicting generalization from circuits (CS 6966, Interpretability of LLMs, Spring 2026)

With Christina Dong and Emma Brown, in Prof. Ana Marasović's course, I asked whether sparse feature circuits extracted from a test LLM (Gemma-2-2B) can predict if its behavior on a default task will carry over to a counterfactual one, with ChatGPT as the analysis model that reads the circuits. On two tasks, a currency analogy and two-hop reasoning, the analysis model predicted no generalization and was right. The two-hop task showed almost none: 7 of 100 counterfactual instances correct. The circuits added little signal beyond the task description alone. We presented the work as a poster in April 2026.

Theme tie. Security and health both depend on knowing when a model's behavior will hold outside the cases you tested; this project measured exactly that. Confirm this framing matches how you see it.

RePurposeRx: rare-disease drug repurposing prototype (Feb 2025)

A prototype that pairs a SwiftUI iOS app with a Flask backend running DeepSeek 7B through Ollama, and draws on public biomedical sources (DrugBank, PubChem, ChEMBL, RxNorm, UniProt, KEGG, Reactome, ClinicalTrials.gov, PubMed) to help researchers and clinicians generate drug-repurposing hypotheses for rare diseases. Its outputs are hypotheses to check, not medical advice.

Video Mind Palace: long-term embodied QA (Jan–May 2026)

Embodied agents answer questions about spaces they explored earlier. Scene-graph memory is expensive to build. I developed a method that queries video directly with a vision-language model, cutting inference time 31–57% while staying within 13–17% of the scene-graph baseline's accuracy. A paper with Advanced AI is in preparation.

STARS Lab, undergraduate researcher (Aug 2025–Feb 2026)

I built a graph-augmented pipeline that extracted structured data from 1,000+ materials-science papers, plus a constraint-based feasibility engine and Ref-RAG, a chatbot over PDF corpora. The lab works with NASA, Microsoft, and the U.S. DoD.

AccessLens: AI in education and accessibility (Sept 2026, first place)

With four teammates on Team Mindstone, I built AccessLens in 48 hours at the University of Utah's Minds and Machines: AI in Education Hackathon. It turns one instructor-approved lesson feed into synchronized visuals, structured text, captions, audio, dyslexia-friendly formatting, and spatial AR, so students with accommodations can learn the way that works for them, privately. Under the hood it is a real-time AWS system: WebSocket sync through API Gateway, Lambda, and DynamoDB; captions from Amazon Transcribe; Claude on Amazon Bedrock for contextual catch-up; Polly and Translate for spoken and multilingual access. The design principle was that the student controls the format and the instructor controls the content. We won first place ($1,500 and $1,000 in AWS credit).
